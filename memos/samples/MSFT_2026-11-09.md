## Summary

Microsoft passes most of the screening criteria for FY2022-FY2026 but fails FCF conversion and FCF yield. Revenue grew at a CAGR of 13.7%[^1], and the operating margin rose from 42.1%[^4] in FY2022 to 46.8%[^6] in FY2026. The balance sheet shows net cash in every year, and interest coverage is 50.88x[^8] in FY2026. However, free cash flow has not kept pace with net income: FCF conversion over the period is 71.9%[^10], and the FCF yield at the current market capitalisation of $4,025,732.6m[^33] is 1.7%[^36].

## Findings

- Revenue rose from $198,270m[^2] in FY2022 to $331,839m[^3] in FY2026, a CAGR of 13.7%[^1]. Growth was uneven: it slowed from 18.0%[^67] in FY2022 to 6.9%[^70] in FY2023 (a change of -11.1 pp[^163]), then recovered to 15.7%[^72] in FY2024 and reached 17.8%[^76] in FY2026.
- Operating margin dipped slightly in FY2023 (-0.3 pp[^164]), then rose every year after that, reaching 46.8%[^6] in FY2026. Operating income grew from $83,383m[^5] to $155,237m[^7].
- Free cash flow was positive in every year but moved within a fairly narrow range, from $65,149m[^12] in FY2022 to $66,987m[^24] in FY2026. Over the same period, net income rose from $72,738m[^28] to $133,749m[^32]. This gap explains the FCF conversion of 71.9%[^10], which is below the threshold.
- ROIC is still well above the threshold at 35.9%[^37] in FY2026, but it fell every year from 79.9%[^84] in FY2022.
- The company held net cash in every year: net debt was -$36,549m[^58] in FY2026, and net debt / EBITDA was -0.19x[^57]. Diluted share count fell slightly, from 7,540.0m[^62] to 7,453.0m[^63], a CAGR of -0.3%[^61].

## Risks

- Free cash flow is growing much more slowly than reported earnings. If that gap continues, reported profits may overstate the cash available to shareholders.
- At 1.7%[^36], the free cash flow yield relative to the market capitalisation of $4,025,732.6m[^33] is below the screening threshold. The current valuation leaves a thin cash return on today's free cash flow.
- ROIC has fallen steadily from 79.9%[^84] to 35.9%[^37]. Invested capital appears to be growing faster than after-tax operating profit, and that trend has not been investigated.
- Net cash has varied considerably from year to year, between -$23,913m[^138] and -$64,025m[^137]. These figures alone do not show what drove the movements.

## Open questions

- Why has free cash flow stayed roughly flat while net income grew from $72,738m[^28] to $133,749m[^32]? How much of the gap comes from capital expenditure, and how much from working capital or non-cash items?
- What explains the slowdown in revenue growth to 6.9%[^70] in FY2023 and the recovery to 15.7%[^72] in FY2024? Did acquisitions, currency effects or particular segments contribute?
- What is driving the steady decline in ROIC: acquisitions, goodwill, capital spending on infrastructure, or a change in how invested capital is defined?
- Has management given guidance on future capital spending and on when it expects free cash flow conversion to recover?
- What accounts for the year-to-year changes in net cash, for example acquisitions, buybacks, dividends or debt repayment?
- What date and share count were used to calculate the market capitalisation of $4,025,732.6m[^33]? Does the FCF yield of 1.7%[^36] change materially with a different reference date?
