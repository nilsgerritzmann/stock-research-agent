## Summary

The balance sheet held a net cash position in every year, free cash flow was positive every year, and diluted share count growth passed with a CAGR of -2.1%[^12]. Return on invested capital passed in FY2026 at 23.8%[^15], but that is down from 61.0%[^37] in FY2022. Free cash flow yield of 3.7%[^59] fails its threshold. Interest coverage was not assessed because the company reports no gross interest expense.

## Findings

- Revenue dropped from $51,362m[^93] in FY2024 to $46,309m[^94] in FY2025, a growth rate of -9.8%[^102]. It was then roughly flat in FY2026 at $46,398m[^3] (0.2%[^104]). The FY2026 growth-swing flag (+10.0 pp[^178]) reflects a return to flat revenue after the FY2025 decline, not a return to growth.
- Operating margin fell by -4.6 pp[^176] in FY2025, from 12.7%[^116] to 8.2%[^117], and stayed close to that level in FY2026 at 8.3%[^8]. Operating income is derived here, not reported: it is calculated as income before taxes minus net interest income, because NIKE does not report an operating income line.
- Free cash flow was positive in every year (0[^86] negative years), and conversion over the full period was 92.3%[^66] of net income. However, annual free cash flow fell from $6,617m[^74] in FY2024 to $3,268m[^77] in FY2025 and $2,184m[^60] in FY2026. In FY2026 it was below net income of $3,108m[^85].
- NIKE had more cash than debt throughout the period. Net debt was -$3,567m[^152] in FY2022 and -$1,085m[^88] in FY2026, and net debt / EBITDA was -0.24x[^87] in FY2026. The net cash position is smaller now than in FY2022.
- The diluted share count fell from 1,610.8m[^13] to 1,481.0m[^14]. Most of that decline came before FY2026: the count was 1,487.6m[^175] in FY2025 and changed only slightly in FY2026.

## Risks

- Profitability is down sharply. ROIC fell from 61.0%[^37] to 23.8%[^15], net income fell from $6,046m[^81] to $3,108m[^85], and FY2026 does not show a clear margin recovery.
- Free cash flow has declined for the last two fiscal years and has fallen below net income. The pass on full-period FCF conversion depends heavily on the stronger earlier years, especially FY2024.
- Free cash flow yield of 3.7%[^59] rests on an approximate market capitalisation of $58,847.5m[^63]. That figure uses FY2026 diluted weighted-average shares because the cover page gives no single current share count across share classes. A different share count would change the yield.
- Interest coverage could not be calculated: gross interest expense is not reported, and the FY2022 input is missing. Debt-servicing capacity is therefore judged only on the net cash position, not on a coverage ratio.

## Open questions

- What does the FY2025 10-K say caused the revenue decline to $46,309m[^94], and how was it split across regions, product categories and sales channels?
- What drove the operating margin decline of -4.6 pp[^176] in FY2025: gross margin, selling and administrative expense, restructuring charges, or something else? Were any of these items one-off?
- Why did free cash flow fall to $2,184m[^60] in FY2026? Did working capital (for example inventory or receivables) or capital expenditure change, and does management expect this to continue?
- How much gross interest expense and total debt does NIKE carry, and on what terms, given that the filings show only net interest income?
- Why did the share count fall more slowly in FY2026? Does the 10-K or earnings call indicate a change in buyback or dividend policy?
- What is the current share count across all share classes, so the market capitalisation and the 3.7%[^59] free cash flow yield can be recalculated more precisely?
